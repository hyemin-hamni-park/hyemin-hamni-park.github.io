Hyemin “Hamni” Park

Junior Fellow at the Turin Humanities Programme, Fondazione 1563 per l’Arte e la Cultura

My name is Hyemin “Hamni” Park, and I am a Junior Fellow at Turin Humanities Programme, Fondazione 1563 per l’Arte e la Cultura. I earned my Ph.D. and M.A. from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and M.A. and B.A. from Sungkyunkwan University.

As a political theorist whose research centers on the comparative study of nineteenth- and twentieth-century Chinese, Japanese, and Korean political thought, I ask how western political concepts and theories, such as the idea of enlightenment, the concept of liberty, and classical liberalism, were translated and woven into Northeast Asian languages through their local uses in the context of nineteenth- and twentieth-century imperialism. My dissertation, Between Hope and Despair: Imagining Freedom in Modern Japan, China, and Korea, examines how the concept of “freedom” 自由 emerged from discourses across Japanese, Chinese, and Korean languages from the 1860s to the 1920s in conjunction with local concepts in transformation, such as civilization and enlightenment. This investigation centers on how Fukuzawa Yukichi (1835–1901), Sun Yat-sen (1866–1925), and Yi Kwangsu (1892–1950) talked about the term “freedom” and talked with the term—especially about themselves, their nations, or those they considered other. I am currently developing my dissertation into a book manuscript. My research is inspired by later Wittgenstein’s philosophy of language and driven by her commitment to human freedom unbound by the limits of what one is given, including language and tradition, and to the possibility of building a common world across differences.
