Hyemin “Hamni” Park

Junior Fellow, Turin Humanities Programme at Fondazione 1563 per l’Arte e la Cultura

My name is Hyemin [hjɛ:min] “Hamni” Park. I am a political theorist working on the history of Northeast Asian political thought. Starting this November, I will be a Junior Fellow at the Turin Humanities Programme, Fondazione 1563 per l’Arte e la Cultura. I received my Ph.D. from the University of Wisconsin-Madison, and my B.A. and M.A. from Sungkyunkwan University in Seoul, South Korea.

My research centers on the comparative study of the nineteenth- and twentieth-century Chinese, Japanese, and Korean political thought. In particular, I am interested in how western political concepts and theories, such as the concept of liberty and classical liberalism, were translated into Japanese, Chinese, and Korean languages in the context of imperialism. My research is driven by my commitment to human freedom unbound by the limits of what one is given, including language and tradition, and the possibilities of building a common world across differences.

I can be reached at hpark362[at]wisc[dot]edu.
